The money nobody is watching

30%of one year's net payer revenue was underpaid or denied for fixable reasons at a four-physician orthopedic practice in Miami-Dade. 1,892 claims, checked line by line.
1–3%what the industry average says commercial payers underpay. It's held down by hospital systems with teams watching.
90–180 daysuntil each underpaid claim becomes unrecoverable

Yes, thirty percent. An independent practice usually has nobody checking finished business against contracted rates, and payers make a short payment look like a paid claim. It's pure margin, too. The care was already delivered and the staff already paid.

And there's a clock. Dispute windows run 90 to 180 days. Whatever sits unnoticed past that is gone for good.

How it works

First, we read your remittances.

Every line, against the contracted rate for that code, payer by payer. A person on our team does this, with software doing the arithmetic. No practice of 3 to 15 doctors staffs that job, and honestly it wouldn't make sense to. Chasing one $180 shortfall never pays for the hour. Finding the four hundred like it does.

About Clearia, for the skeptic

A firm, not a software product

People do this work. Someone on our team reads your remittances line by line, builds each appeal package, and tracks the payer until the money lands. There is software behind it, plenty of it, but you never install anything or learn a dashboard. You talk to the person doing the work.

Small, Florida, and picky about engagements

Clearia, Inc. is a Florida corporation based in Miami. We work with independent specialty practices in South Florida first, because payer rules, workers' comp fee schedules, and appeal deadlines are state-specific and we know Florida's cold. We take an engagement only when your own data shows real recoverable dollars. If it doesn't, we tell you that and stop.

What we will not do

  • Touch your billing, your posting, or your payer contracts.
  • Receive or hold any of your money. Payers keep paying you the same way they do today.
  • Send anything to a payer your team hasn't approved.
  • Ask for patient data by email. Everything runs under a signed business associate agreement with read-only access.
"Why haven't I heard of you?"

Because we're new and small, and we've chosen to grow one practice at a time in one region rather than advertise nationally. That's also why the diagnostic is free: it's how we earn the right to the engagement.

"Is this a collections agency?"

No. Nothing we do touches patients or patient balances. We work on the payer side only, through the reconsideration and appeal processes your contracts already provide.

"What's the catch on the free diagnostic?"

There isn't one, and here's the honest logic. Most practices that see the number in their own data want it recovered, so the diagnostic pays for itself often enough. When it shows nothing, you get a clean read of your payers and we move on.

"Who do I actually deal with?"

The same two or three people from the first call through the last dollar. No handoff to an offshore team, no ticket queue.
