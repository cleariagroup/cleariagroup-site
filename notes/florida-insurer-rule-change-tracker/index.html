Insurer payment rule changes for Florida orthopedic practices: the tracker

By F Cantor, Clearia · Published November 20, 2026 · Updated November 20, 2026 · Covers changes through October 31, 2026

Five insurers changed the rules for getting paid on an orthopedic claim 84 times in 24 months, about once every nine days. That's UnitedHealthcare, Aetna, Cigna, Florida Blue and Humana, November 2024 to October 2026, counting only changes each insurer published item by item. This page lists every one in plain words, with dates and the source, and we update it every month.

Practices feel it. In Experian Health's 2026 State of Claims survey, 78 percent of providers said insurers change their payment policies more often than before. Our count puts a number on it for Florida orthopedics.

Each change can start a new run of short payments or denials the day it takes effect. Nobody sends the practice a list. Someone has to read every bulletin, work out which claims it touches, and change how the practice bills before the payments come back short.

What changed most

Most changes are about which procedures still get paid separately and how claims and appeals must be sent. Only a few are about office visit levels, but those touch the most claims.

Five changes that land on an orthopedic practice

  • Two scopes on the same shoulder. UnitedHealthcare stopped paying them separately in early 2025, and the usual modifier no longer gets around it.
  • A new arthroscopy formula, applied backward. Florida Blue posted a new payment method for multiple arthroscopic procedures on February 13, 2025, effective January 1. Under its current table, one second-scope shoulder procedure pays 10.5% of its normal fee.
  • Office visits paid a level lower. Cigna's visit coding accuracy policy, effective October 1, 2025, pays some visits a level down when it decides the record doesn't support the level billed. Getting paid as billed means sending the visit note.
  • Laminectomy with a spinal osteotomy. Aetna stopped paying nine laminectomy codes separately when billed with a posterior spinal osteotomy, effective April 1, 2026, announced the same month.
  • X-ray readings on visit days. UnitedHealthcare stopped paying the reading of an X-ray billed the same day as a visit by the same provider unless the report is attached, effective April 1, 2026.

The timing problem

Of the 34 changes where both the posting date and the effective date were public, 12 took effect the day they were posted or earlier. Half, 17, gave 30 days or less. And 84 is a floor: another 41 policies were marked "revised" or "updated" with no public detail on what changed.

What this means for your practice

  • Assume the rules moved since your last check. At this pace, a quarter brings around ten changes across these five insurers.
  • Check payments by pattern from each effective date. A change shows up as the same code at the same insurer paying short, again and again. Our guide to spotting an underpayment walks through the check.
  • Watch office visit levels. Visit-level reviews pay less without issuing a denial. See how payer downcoding works.
  • Mind the appeal clock. Every short payment has a window. Here are the Florida appeal deadlines by insurer.

How we count

One researcher per insurer read each one's public provider bulletins, newsletters, reimbursement policy updates, policy revision logs and provider manuals for November 1, 2024 through October 31, 2026. We count rules about payment, coding, documentation, claims and appeals that apply to orthopedic professional claims, commercial and Medicare Advantage. Clinical and medical necessity criteria, prior authorization lists, pharmacy, facility-only and pure rate updates are left out. Each change is counted once, at first notice, and dated at the earlier of its announcement and effective dates. Changes posted without public detail, or with detail only behind a provider portal, are not in the 84.

Questions people ask

How often do insurers change their payment rules?

At the five largest insurers for Florida orthopedic practices, the rules for getting paid on an orthopedic claim changed 84 times between November 2024 and October 2026, about once every nine days. That counts only changes each insurer published item by item. Another 41 policies were marked revised or updated with no public detail, so 84 is a floor.

How much notice do insurers give before a payment rule changes?

Often very little. Of the 34 changes where both the posting date and the effective date were public, 12 took effect the day they were posted or earlier, and 17 gave 30 days or less.

How do we know if a rule change cost our practice money?

Compare what each insurer paid against your contract and fee schedule for the claims a change touches, starting from its effective date. A change usually shows up as a pattern of short payments or denials for the same code at the same insurer, not as a single claim.

Where do insurers publish payment rule changes?

In their provider bulletins and newsletters, reimbursement policy updates, policy revision logs and provider manuals. Some detail sits behind each insurer's provider portal, which is why part of what changed is not visible publicly.
